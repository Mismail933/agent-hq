# Story guide: long narrated stories (The Infographics Show model)

Written by the Builder on 2026-10-17 from the 20 most-viewed videos of The Infographics Show (15.5M subscribers), their
transcripts read in full in the browser (the player's own caption file; nothing downloaded). It is what Calina follows for a
`story_v1` project and what Israa checks against. **We learn their craft; we never copy their words, titles, characters or
branding.** Every script is our own research and our own sentences.

## 2. The shape of a script

Narration runs at about 195-215 words a minute. The 20 run 1,400-5,800 words (7-28 minutes). The story sets the length.

1. **Cold open (first 20-40 seconds): drop the viewer into the most intense moment.** Present tense, often "you": a gunshot
   and then the blood on your shirt; diggers freezing in a tunnel as a patrol truck passes overhead; a cook in his underwear
   in a capsized boat. No "Did you know", no greeting. Sensory detail: sound, smell, darkness, a clock.
2. **The title question, said out loud**, then the promise: "let's find out", or "first the story, then what we dug up".
3. **Context in one breath**, with one hard number early (a poll percentage, a count, a date).
4. **A spine the viewer can feel moving**:
   - a **clock** ("3:33 AM", "00:05", "8 p.m.", "Day 5", "Hour 3"): the strongest format, used in 4 of the top 20;
   - or a **life in order** (childhood, first crime, the mistake, the arrest);
   - or a **countdown list** (#15 to #1, worst last);
   - or **legend, then the test**.
5. **Re-hook every 60-90 seconds** with an open loop or a turn:
   - a one-line turn that tells the viewer the plan is about to break (in our own words every time);
   - "we'll come back to him" (a promise kept later);
   - a cliffhanger at the end of a section, then switch to the other side of the story (the divers, the police, the patrol).
   - two witnesses who disagree: say both, say which one the record supports.
6. **Numbers you can see**: every stat made physical ("taller than a two-storey house", "heavy enough to flatten a car",
   feet AND metres). These are the on-screen moments.
7. **Real voices**: a diary line, a survivor, a guard, an after-action report, a court record. Short, attributed, real.
8. **Dry humour as relief, never at a victim's expense**: a deadpan aside after a grim fact, a pop-culture comparison, a
   wink at the channel itself. Roughly one light line a minute in dark material.
9. **The turn near the end**: the twist, the debunk, what happened afterwards, the irony (the inventor of the bull killed in
   his own bull; the most-wanted man caught because of a stolen car report).
10. **A short ending**: one strong last line (a quote or an irony), one question to the comments that people want to answer
    ("would you have volunteered?"), and one line naming the next video to watch.

## 3. The voice

- One narrator, warm and conversational, "we" for the channel and "you" for the viewer. Plain words.
- Sentences of mixed length: a long one that builds, then a short one that lands.
- Dramatised scenes are allowed, but **say when you are imagining**: "picture this", "here we mix fact with fiction",
  "that story is true". Invented characters (a couple at the end of the world) are fine in a what-if, named and clearly
  invented.
- They **visibly hold back** on gore: "we won't go into the details". So do we.

## 4. Our rules on top (the company's, not theirs)

- **Advertiser-safe**: no graphic gore, no sexual content, no drug how-tos, no glorifying a killer, respect for victims (name
  them with care, never joke about them). The dark topic is the hook; the facts and the human story carry it.
- **Real sources for every fact**, quoted. A claim nobody can source is cut or said as "according to his own account".
- **No copying**: never reuse their titles, sentences, characters, thumbnails or channel name. Topic families are common
  ground; their scripts are not.
- **Our own cast**: our main character and his world are ours (made with Fable), drawn in our own flat style.
- Write for the picture: every section says what the screen shows (our main character in the scene, a map, a gauge filling, a clock, a
  number counting up, a diagram).

## 5. Checklist (Calina writes against it, Israa checks it)

1. The first 30 seconds drop us into a moment, in the present tense. No greeting, no "did you know".
2. The title question is said out loud within the first minute.
3. A spine you can name: clock, life in order, countdown, or legend-then-test.
4. A re-hook (open loop, turn or cliffhanger) at least every 90 seconds.
5. At least one hard number a minute, each made physical, each sourced.
6. At least three real voices (quotes) with their source.
7. Humour present, never about a victim.
8. A real turn near the end.
9. An ending with one strong line, one comment question, one next-video line.
10. Every section has a `shows` line the animator can draw.
11. Advertiser-safe and honest: imagined scenes are labelled, nothing graphic.
