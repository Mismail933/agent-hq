## The tone

- **Dry and a little smug.** The channel calls its own narrator "a very smug squire". He knows how this ends and enjoys it. He never sounds
  excited and never oversells. The facts are the jokes.
- **Understatement.** The bigger the absurdity, the flatter the delivery. Horror and heroism are both stated plainly.
- **Talk to the viewer or point at the person.** "You" (you are this soldier) or "this man / this pilot". Never "today we'll look at", no
  greeting, no "did you know".
- **Flowing sentences joined by cause.** Each beat follows from the last with but / so / then. Avoid runs of fragments: the flop is written
  in fragments with ellipses, the hits mostly in full sentences. This matches our own rule in the even-voice work (2.74.0).
- **Short sentences only as punches.** A one- or two-word sentence is saved for a turn or the last line.
- **Plain words.** A curious teenager understands every sentence first time, and every number is something you can picture.

## The shape of the script

1. **First line = the whole paradox**, with a named or pointed-at person and the stakes, inside about 3-5 seconds.
2. **A chain of but / so**, about one beat every sentence or two, each one with something new to draw.
3. **A turn around two-thirds in**: the moment it gets worse or stranger.
4. **The last line lands the payoff**, often on one word or a fact that reverses what you assumed. No "subscribe", no summary.

Length: 180-250 words, about 60-75 seconds.

## What not to do

Mood openings. Rhetorical build-up. Excitement in the voice. Jokes that mock victims (our standing rule: dry humour is never about victims).
A smug tone on stories where it would be wrong, such as slavery or atrocity: there, keep the plain delivery and drop the smirk.
